## Objective

The main objective of this project is to produce a complete Project Management Plan for a proposed IT project: a single document that carries the project from justification through to closure, applying every knowledge area covered in this course.

By the end of this project, you should be able to take a real organizational need, decide whether it is worth doing, define what will and will not be built, break the work down, schedule it, cost it, staff it, and plan for the things that will go wrong.

---

## Instructions

You will work in your group. Each group acts as the project management team for one proposed IT project and produces a single Project Management Plan document, followed by a presentation to the class.

### Choosing your project

Your project must be for an organization/business with a real problem. You are planning the project, not building the system. No code is required or expected.

Acceptable sources of a project:

- An office, department or student organization here in CICT or WVSU
- A barangay, LGU office, cooperative, parish, school or clinic you have access to
- A small business: a shop, carinderia, printing service, rental, farm supply
- An NGO or community group

Your project must be large enough to need managing with roughly six to twelve months of work and a budget you can defend, but small enough that your group could plausibly deliver it.

### Checkpoint 1 is a proposal, not a plan

Do not write or start the full document until you have passed the proposal with the following:

Before you write anything else, submit a **one-page project concept**: the organization, the problem, the proposed system, and who your sponsor would possibly be. Pass this **on or before October 14, 2026**. (Arial 11, A4)

One PDF per group, plus a presentation. The document contains the following key deliverables:

## Template

Use these section numbers and headings. Sections marked with a diamond (◆) carry the heaviest marks.

### 1. Title Page

- Project title and the client organization
- Submitted by: group name, all member names, section
- Submitted to: your instructor and the named project sponsor
- Date of submission

### 2. Executive Summary

- One page maximum. The problem, the proposed system, the cost, the duration, and what the organization gains.
- Written LAST, but placed here.
- Assume the reader will read nothing else.

### 3. ◆ Project Background and Business Case

- The organization: what it does, how big it is, how it currently handles the problem.
- The problem, quantified where you can: hours lost, forms misplaced, people queueing, revenue foregone.
- Options considered, including doing nothing.
- Your recommendation and why.

### 4. ◆ Project Charter

- Use the standard charter structure: title and authorization date, project manager, start and finish dates, key milestones, budget, objectives, success criteria, approach, roles and responsibilities, sign-off and stakeholder comments.
- Success criteria must be measurable. "Users will be satisfied" is not a criterion.

### 5. Stakeholder Register and Analysis

- A table of at least eight stakeholders: name or role, relationship to the project, what they want from it, and their level of influence with a justification.
- At least one must be someone who would prefer the project did not happen.
- A short paragraph on how you would manage that person.

### 6. ◆ Scope Statement

- Product scope description · deliverables · user acceptance criteria.
- Project boundaries: an explicit list of what is NOT included.
- Constraints and assumptions.
- Version number and date. A scope statement without a version is not being maintained.

### 7. ◆ Work Breakdown Structure

- A WBS decomposed to at least three levels, in either chart or indented-list form, with consistent numbering.
- A WBS dictionary entry for at least five work packages: what is included, what is not, who owns it, and what it produces.
- Include a 1.0 Project Management branch. Managing the project is work.

### 8. ◆ Schedule

- An activity list derived from your WBS work packages, with duration estimates and a stated basis for those estimates.
- A network diagram showing dependencies, with the critical path identified.
- A Gantt chart with milestones marked.
- State the software used.

### 9. ◆ Cost Estimate and Budget

- A cost estimate by WBS item, with the estimating method named: analogous, parametric or bottom-up.
- A cost baseline showing cumulative cost over time.
- Assumptions behind the figures. Peso amounts, with separators.

### 10. Quality Management Plan

- What "acceptable" means for your main deliverables, stated in measurable terms.
- How quality will be assured during the work and controlled at the end.
- Who accepts each deliverable.

### 11. Resource and Responsibility Plan

- A project organizational chart showing the sponsor, the project manager, and the team.
- A responsibility assignment matrix mapping WBS items to people.
- Skills required, and any skills the team does not have.

### 12. Communications Plan

- A table of who receives what information, in what format, how often, and from whom.
- Escalation path: who is told when something goes wrong, and how fast.
- How stakeholder feedback is captured and acted on.

### 13. ◆ Risk Register

- At least eight risks, ranked, each with a probability and impact rating, an owner, and a planned response.
- At least three must be about people or the organization, not technology.
- Name your top risk and explain why it is top.

### 14. Procurement Plan

- What will be bought or outsourced, and what will be done in-house.
- How a supplier would be selected: criteria and process.
- What you would insist on in the contract to protect the client.

### 15. Change Control and Closing Plan

- How change requests will be raised, assessed, decided and recorded.
- Who sits on the change control board.
- Closing: acceptance, turnover, lessons learned, and the transition plan naming who maintains the system afterwards.

### 16. Conclusion

- The value the project delivers, in the client's terms.
- What you are asking the sponsor to approve.
- One paragraph on what your group found hardest to plan, and why.

### 17. Appendices

- Evidence of client contact: interview notes, photos, correspondence, or a letter.
- Any forms, screenshots or documents from the current process.
- Individual contribution statement signed by all five members.

## Rubrics

Total: 100 points (5 criteria × 20 points).

### 1. Project Initiation and Business Case (20)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent (20)** | Business case is quantified and compelling, with options considered. Charter is complete and formal, with measurable success criteria and sign-off. Stakeholder register has 8+ entries including a genuine opponent, with justified influence ratings. |
| **Very Good (15)** | Business case is clear but justification lacks quantification. Charter is complete. Stakeholder register is complete but influence ratings are asserted rather than justified. |
| **Good (10)** | Charter is present but missing key elements such as sponsor, success criteria or sign-off. Problem is vaguely defined. Fewer than 8 stakeholders, or no opponent identified. |
| **Developing (0–5)** | Charter absent or unusable. Business case missing or generic. Stakeholder analysis absent. |

### 2. Scope Management and WBS (20)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent (20)** | Scope statement is precise, with explicit IN and OUT boundaries, acceptance criteria, constraints and assumptions. WBS is 3+ levels, deliverable-oriented, and satisfies the 100% rule. Dictionary entries are complete for 5+ work packages. |
| **Very Good (15)** | Scope is clearly defined with boundaries. WBS is 3+ levels but has minor decomposition inconsistencies. Dictionary present but thin. |
| **Good (10)** | Scope lacks explicit OUT boundaries. WBS is too shallow, missing major deliverables, or mixes tasks with deliverables. Dictionary incomplete. |
| **Developing (0–5)** | Scope undefined. WBS absent or fundamentally incorrect: a task list rather than a deliverable breakdown. |

### 3. Schedule and Cost (20)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent (20)** | Activity list derives from the WBS. Network diagram shows dependencies with the critical path identified and justified. Gantt chart has milestones. Cost estimate names its method, rolls up to a baseline, and states assumptions. |
| **Very Good (15)** | Schedule is realistic with milestones and dependencies. Critical path discernible but not justified. Budget is realistic but lacks itemized justification. |
| **Good (10)** | Schedule present but uses unrealistic estimates or is missing dependencies and milestones. Budget provided but generic, or not traceable to the WBS. |
| **Developing (0–5)** | Schedule absent or unusable for tracking. Budget absent or containing critical errors. |

### 4. Quality, Resource, Communications and Risk (20)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent (20)** | Quality criteria are measurable and assigned an acceptor. Org chart and responsibility matrix map cleanly to the WBS. Communications plan specifies audience, format, frequency and escalation. 8+ risks ranked and owned, at least 3 non-technical, with real responses. |
| **Very Good (15)** | All four plans present and workable. Risks identified with suitable responses but some are generic. Responsibility matrix present but not fully mapped to the WBS. |
| **Good (10)** | One plan is missing or superficial. Fewer than 8 risks, or responses are generic. Quality criteria stated but not measurable. |
| **Developing (0–5)** | Two or more plans missing. Risk management absent or reduced to a list of worries with no owners. |

### 5. Procurement, Closing and Proposal Quality (20)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent (20)** | Procurement decisions justified with selection criteria and a named contract safeguard. Change control and closing plans are concrete, including a transition plan naming who maintains the system. Document is professional, consistent and flawless. Presentation is clear and delivered by the whole group. |
| **Very Good (15)** | Procurement and closing addressed adequately. Document is well written with minor organizational flaws. Presentation is competent but uneven across members. |
| **Good (10)** | Procurement or closing treated superficially. No transition plan. Document lacks professional polish: inconsistent formatting, unreadable diagrams. Presentation dominated by one or two members. |
| **Developing (0–5)** | Procurement and closing missing. Document poorly written, disorganized or incomplete. Presentation not delivered by the group. |

**TOTAL: 100 points**
