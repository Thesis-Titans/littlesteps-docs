## 16.1 The Value to the Client

For the CSWDO La Paz–Lapuz District office, LittleSteps gives parents in its 37 barangays a free, private way to keep track of their child's learning at home and to find short, screen-free activities that use what they already have. It does this without diagnostic scores, without selling data and without adding reporting work for Child Development Workers. In the planning estimates, each center saves about 80 CDW hours and ₱4,500 in printing a year, and fewer families lose their child's home record. It also gives the city a tested, low-cost tool for meeting its new duties under the ECCD System Act that it can extend to its other districts.

## 16.3 What Was Hardest to Plan

The hardest part was planning for a client we did not contact. We chose the CSWDO La Paz–Lapuz District office from public information, so the city and district facts are real, but every per-center figure (how many families have phones, how many notebooks are lost, how long CDWs spend on handouts) is our own estimate. That made the business case and the success criteria harder to pin down, because their baselines are assumptions. We handled it by labelling every estimate, saying how the real project would measure it in its first two weeks, and making the success criteria relative to that baseline. A second difficulty was the product's own boundary: an app about child development that must never judge a child. It shaped the scope statement's exclusions, the quality plan's wording rule and the content review step, and it is why the catalog is on the plan's longest non-technical path.
