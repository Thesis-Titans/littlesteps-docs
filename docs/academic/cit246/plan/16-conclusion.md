# 16. Conclusion

## 16.1 The Value to the Client

For the CSWDO La Paz–Lapuz District office, LittleSteps gives parents in its 37 barangays a free, private way to keep track of their child's learning at home and to find short, screen-free activities that use what they already have. It does this without diagnostic scores, without selling data and without adding reporting work for Child Development Workers. At the planning estimates the app could free up to about 80 CDW hours and ₱4,500 in printing per center each year, and fewer families would lose their child's home record. The charter's target is more modest: a 50% cut in handout time. The city also gains a tested, low-cost tool, usable for its duties under the ECCD System Act (RA 12199), that it could extend to other districts.

## 16.3 What Was Hardest to Plan

The hardest part was setting baselines from limited contact. We wrote to the district office, met the OIC District Head and interviewed two CDWs and two parents, but four interviews cannot settle every per-center figure (families with phones, notebooks lost, CDW time on handouts). We kept each figure as a labelled planning figure, stated how the project would measure it in its first two weeks, and made the success criteria relative to that baseline. A second difficulty was the product's boundary: an app about child development that must never judge a child. It shaped the scope exclusions, the quality plan's wording rule and the content review step, and it is why the catalog is on the longest non-technical path.
